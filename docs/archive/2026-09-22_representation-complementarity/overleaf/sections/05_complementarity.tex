For CNN versus HistGB, the daily cross-sectional score correlation is about 0.37 and top-decile overlap is about 26\%. The overlap is higher than random but far from complete. Fama--MacBeth regressions and conditional double sorts indicate two-way incremental predictive content.

For CNN versus the validation-selected MLP-4 Bag10 benchmark, the score correlation is 0.338 and top-decile overlap is 23\%. In joint Fama--MacBeth regressions, both scores retain positive coefficients: the CNN coefficient conditional on MLP-4 is 0.00071 ($t=5.75$), while the MLP-4 coefficient conditional on CNN is 0.00149 ($t=10.85$). Thus, the stronger flattened learner does not eliminate CNN-specific information.

These results motivate a decomposition of predictions into shared and model-specific components. Similar input data and similar average performance do not imply that models learn identical economic signals.
